Summary

Open conveyor lines have the largest product exposure area on the production floor. This article explains why conveyor risk far exceeds filling and mixing equipment through the triple stack of exposure area, exposure time, and self-generated dust, and provides segmented tape testing and protection solutions based on the ELVONIS Four Layers.

1. The Phenomenon: Product Runs Naked

Fillers have covers. Mixers have lids. Conveyors are open. Product travels dozens of metres. Dozens of metres of exposure risk. The auditor walks the length of the conveyor, crouches to look under the belt, and aims a flashlight at the rollers and guide rails—they see a complete contamination pathway.

2. Common Misconception

Conveyor lines receive far less attention than fillers. Most plants clean only the start and end of the line, while the middle section is neglected for months. A more insidious error is ignoring the conveyor's own dust generation: rubber microparticles from belt-roller friction, metal micro-shavings from chain guide friction, and carbon powder from drive motor brush wear. The conveyor is not just a pathway for product—it is also manufacturing its own dust.

3. Engineering Principle: The Triple Stack

Factor one: exposure area. Product on a conveyor is directly exposed; the exposure area equals the entire open area above the line. A 30-metre conveyor with a 0.5-metre width has an exposure area of 15 square metres—no filler or mixer can provide such a large open exposure surface.

Factor two: exposure time. Product residence time on the line ranges from dozens of seconds to several minutes. During this entire period, product remains exposed to plant air. Larger exposure area plus longer exposure time equals higher probability of particle settling onto product.

Factor three: self-generated dust. The conveyor is both a deposition surface and a dust source. Belt movement, chain drive, and roller rotation all continuously generate fine particles. In the STADR Model, the conveyor creates the shortest contamination pathway from Source to Audit Risk—product passes directly beside the dust source with no buffer.

4. Field Verification: Segmented Tape Testing

Step one: place tape at five critical points along the conveyor—start, one-quarter, midpoint, three-quarters, and end. Each tape exposed for 24 hours.

Step two: compare deposition rates at all five points and calculate the deposition gradient. If end-point deposition is significantly higher than start-point (e.g., 3× or more), the conveyor travel direction aligns with airflow direction, and dust accumulates progressively along the line.

Step three: white glove wipe test on the conveyor surface. Single-pass wipe along belt edges and roller brackets. Observe the colour and quantity of residue. Grey powder indicates rubber wear. Black powder indicates carbon brush wear. Metallic particles indicate chain guide wear.

Step four: belt friction dust inspection. With the line stopped, touch the roller surface with a finger. If rubber powder adheres to your finger, belt tension is excessive or rollers are worn and require replacement.

5. Engineering Solution: Applying the ELVONIS Four Layers

Layer 1: Source — Elimination. Adjust belt tension to reduce friction-generated dust. Replace worn rollers and chain guides regularly. Inspect drive motor carbon brush condition.

Layer 2: Airflow — Engineering Controls. Never direct supply air straight onto the conveyor. Install return grilles above the conveyor to capture self-generated dust and nearby airborne particles.

Layer 3: Surface — Engineering Controls. Cover wherever possible. This is the most direct and effective measure in conveyor dust control. A semi-enclosed stainless steel cover is the best option—it blocks overhead deposition while retaining operation and observation windows. Install return extraction inside the cover to continuously remove internal dust.

Layer 4: Cleaning — Last Line of Defence. Determine cleaning frequency based on deposition rates from segmented tape testing. Increase frequency in high-deposition sections; reduce in low-deposition sections.

6. Engineering Case Card

Industry: Snack Foods

Finding: A 30-metre open conveyor ran through the packaging area. Auditors found visible dust particles on product surfaces in the middle and end sections, and heavy dust accumulation under the conveyor. Non-conformances were recorded in two consecutive customer audits.

Root Cause

Segmented tape testing showed end-point deposition approximately 3× the start-point. Conveyor travel direction aligned with supply air direction, and airflow continuously transported dust downstream. Simultaneously, belt-roller friction generated rubber microparticles that were not promptly removed. The middle and end sections had long been excluded from the cleaning programme—only the start and end were cleaned.

Engineering Actions

Installed semi-enclosed stainless steel covers over the middle and end sections. Adjusted supply air grille angle from horizontal to 15 degrees upward, avoiding direct blow onto the conveyor. Installed return extraction inside the covers. Adjusted belt tension to specification. Replaced 8 worn rollers.

Results

Deposition dropped over 70%. Product surface dust complaints fell from 5 per month to zero. Zero findings in the next two audits.

Lessons Learned

Covering is the most direct and effective conveyor dust control measure. Self-generated dust is an often-overlooked contamination source; belt tension and roller condition are the key control points. Segmented tape testing precisely locates deposition gradients, guiding differentiated cleaning and protection.

7. First Principle

ELVONIS Principle No.13

Risk equals exposure area multiplied by exposure time. The first control measure for conveyors is isolation, not cleaning.

8. Engineering Takeaways

The triple stack—exposure area, exposure time, and self-generated dust—drives the highest conveyor risk.

Segmented tape testing quantifies deposition gradients and locates high-risk sections.

Covering is the most direct and effective engineering measure.

Airflow control prevents progressive dust accumulation along the line.

Belt tension, rollers, and carbon brushes are the three major self-generated dust sources.

摘要

开放式输送线是车间里产品暴露面积最大的设备。本文从暴露面积、暴露时间和自身产尘三重叠加出发，解释为什么输送线风险远高于灌装和搅拌设备，并提供分段胶带测试法和基于ELVONIS四层控制架构的防护方案。

一、现象：产品在上面裸奔

灌装机有防护罩，搅拌缸有盖子。但输送线是敞开的。产品在上面走了几十米。这几十米，就是几十米的暴露风险。审核员沿着输送线走一圈，蹲下看皮带下方，手电筒照向托辊和导轨——他们看到的是一条完整的污染路径。

二、常见误区

对输送线的关注度远低于灌装机。大多数工厂只在输送线起点和终点做清洁，中间段长期被忽视。更隐蔽的错误是忽略输送线自身产尘——皮带与托辊摩擦产生的橡胶微粒、链条导轨摩擦产生的金属微屑、传动电机碳刷磨损产生的碳粉。输送线不仅是产品的通道，它自己也在制造粉尘。

三、工程原理：三重叠加

因素一：暴露面积。输送线上产品直接裸露，暴露面积等于整条输送线的上方开放面积。一条30米长、0.5米宽的输送线，暴露面积就是15平方米——没有任何灌装机或搅拌缸能提供这么大的开放暴露面。

因素二：暴露时间。产品从上线到下线的停留时间，从几十秒到几分钟不等。在此期间，产品持续暴露在车间空气中。暴露面积越大，暴露时间越长，颗粒沉降到产品上的概率越高。

因素三：自身产尘。输送线既是沉降面，也是尘源。皮带运行、链条传动、托辊滚动都在持续产生微小颗粒。在STADR模型中，输送线在“尘源”和“审核风险”之间建立了一条最短污染路径——产品直接从尘源旁边穿过，没有任何缓冲。

四、现场验证方法：分段胶带测试法

第一步：在输送线五个关键点位放置胶带——起点、四分之一处、中间、四分之三处、终点。每处胶带暴露24小时。

第二步：对比五个点位的沉降量，计算沉降梯度。如果终点沉降量明显高于起点（例如3倍以上），说明输送线运行方向与气流方向一致，粉尘在沿程不断累积。

第三步：输送线表面白手套擦拭检查。单次擦拭皮带边缘和托辊支架，观察灰痕颜色和量。灰色粉末说明橡胶磨损，黑色粉末说明碳刷磨损，金属色说明链条导轨磨损。

第四步：皮带摩擦产尘检查。停机状态下用手触摸托辊表面，如果手指沾有橡胶粉末，说明皮带张力过大或托辊已磨损，需要更换。

五、工程解决方案：ELVONIS四层控制架构

第一层：源头层——消除。调整皮带张力减少摩擦产尘，定期更换磨损托辊和链条导轨，检查传动电机碳刷状态。

第二层：气流层——工程控制。绝对不要将送风口直接对着输送线吹。在输送线上方设置回风口，捕捉输送线自身产生的粉尘和附近扬尘。

第三层：表面层——工程控制。能加盖就加盖。这是输送线粉尘控制中最直接、最有效的手段。半封闭不锈钢防护罩是最佳选择，既能阻挡上方沉降，又保留操作和观察窗口。罩内设置回风吸口，将罩内粉尘及时抽走。

第四层：清洁层——最后防线。基于分段胶带测试的沉降速度确定清洁频率。沉降量大的区段增加清洁频次，沉降量小的区段减少频次。

六、工程案例卡片

行业：休闲食品

发现项：30米开放式输送线贯穿包装区，审核员在输送线中段和终点段发现产品表面有可见粉尘颗粒，输送线下方积尘严重。客户审核中连续两次被记录为不符合项。

根因分析

分段胶带测试显示，终点沉降量约为起点的3倍。输送线运行方向与车间送风方向一致，气流将沿程粉尘持续输送到下游。同时，皮带与托辊摩擦产生橡胶微粒，未被及时清除，进一步加重了污染。输送线中段和终点段长期缺乏清洁覆盖，只有起点和终点被纳入清洁程序。

工程措施

加装半封闭不锈钢防护罩，覆盖输送线中段和终点段。送风口格栅方向从水平改为向上倾斜15度，避免直接吹向输送线。防护罩内设置回风吸口，将罩内粉尘抽走。调整皮带张力至标准值，更换8个磨损托辊。

结果对比

沉降量下降超过70%。产品表面可见粉尘颗粒投诉从每月5起降为0。后续两次审核零发现。

经验教训

加盖防护是最直接有效的输送线粉尘控制手段。输送线自身产尘是常被忽视的污染源，皮带张力和托辊状态是关键控制点。分段胶带测试能精确定位沉降梯度，指导差异化清洁和防护。

七、第一性原理

ELVONIS第一性原理第十三条

风险等于暴露面积乘以暴露时间。输送线控制的第一手段不是清洁，是隔离。

八、工程要点总结

三重叠加——暴露面积、暴露时间、自身产尘——导致输送线风险最高。

分段胶带测试量化沉降梯度，定位高风险区段。

加盖防护是最直接有效的工程措施。

气流控制防止沿程粉尘累积。

皮带张力、托辊和碳刷是输送线自身产尘的三大来源。
